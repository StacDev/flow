`FlowComposer` is the raised input card: a multiline field that grows with
the draft, a send button that becomes **stop** while the model is
generating, and slots for host actions on the design's rail. `onSend` is
required — a composer needs somewhere to send to. The card sits on
`surfaceBright` behind a gradient hairline that firms on hover and focus,
its field a single line with an empty draft — which also drains the send
disc to the disabled ink. The whole card is the field's click target:
a click anywhere on it that is not a control focuses the field, and the
pointer reads as a text cursor over all of it. For simpler flows there is
also a [compact](#compact) layout: a single-row pill that opens into this
card as the draft grows.

## Composed

The composer's slots take other flow_ui components directly — here the
"+" menu in `leadingActions` and the model selector in `trailingActions`:

<FlowDemo demo="composer" height={340} title="The composer" />

```dart title="Send, stop, and the action slots"
FlowComposer(
  placeholder: 'Message flow_ui…',
  isStreaming: generating,
  onSend: (text) => startGeneration(text),
  onStop: cancelGeneration,
  leadingActions: [
    FlowMenu(
      icon: Icons.add,
      tooltip: 'Add to chat',
      entries: [
        FlowMenuOption(
          id: 'photos', icon: Icons.image_outlined, label: 'Photos'),
        FlowMenuOption(
          id: 'files', icon: Icons.upload_file_outlined, label: 'Files'),
      ],
      onSelected: (id) => pickAttachment(id),
    ),
  ],
  trailingActions: [
    FlowModelSelector(
      models: [
        FlowModelOption(id: 'sonnet', label: 'Sonnet 5'),
        FlowModelOption(id: 'opus', label: 'Opus 5'),
      ],
      selectedId: modelId,
      onSelected: (id) => setModel(id),
      efforts: [
        FlowEffortOption(id: 'medium', label: 'Medium'),
        FlowEffortOption(id: 'high', label: 'High'),
      ],
      selectedEffortId: effortId,
      onEffortSelected: (id) => setEffort(id),
    ),
  ],
)
```

## Compact

`layout: FlowComposerLayout.compact` folds the card into a single row: the
attach button and `leadingActions` on the left, the field in the middle,
`trailingActions` and send on the right, and no action row beneath. Only
what you wire is drawn, so the bare form is a field and a send button in a
50px pill.

<FlowDemo demo="composer" variant="compact" height={300} title="The compact composer" />

```dart title="The same composer, one row"
FlowComposer(
  layout: FlowComposerLayout.compact,
  onSend: send,
  onAttachmentsPicked: (picked) => setState(() => pending.addAll(picked)),
  attachTooltip: 'Attach files',
  attachments: pending,
  onRemoveAttachment: removePending,
)
```

Attachments don't change the layout: the pending strip sits above the row
inside the card, and the field stays inline with the controls. The pill
opens into the full card by itself only when the draft needs the room, once
it wraps past one line from a long line or an explicit newline. It folds
back when the draft is empty, which is what a send leaves behind with
`clearOnSend`, so deleting back to one line never moves the controls out
from under the caret. The field keeps its focus and its draft through both.

`layout` is the form the composer takes with an empty draft. To open the
card by hand, from a button of your own, flip it to
`FlowComposerLayout.expanded`.

<Aside type="tip" title="Mind the row on phones">
Everything in a compact composer shares one row, so keep inline actions to
icons on narrow screens. `FlowPill` already drops its label on phones; the
model selector's label is the one to watch.
</Aside>

## Fixed height

The card normally hugs its draft: one line when empty, growing to
`maxLines`. For a composer at a fixed height, set `expands: true` and size
it from the parent. The card fills the height it is given, and the field
takes the space above the action row and scrolls inside it, so `maxLines`
no longer applies.

<FlowDemo demo="composer" variant="fixed" height={340} title="A composer at a fixed height" />

```dart title="Filling a 240px box"
SizedBox(
  height: 240,
  child: FlowComposer(
    expands: true,
    onSend: send,
  ),
)
```

Like `TextField.expands`, it needs a bounded height from its parent, so
inside a `Column` wrap it in `Expanded`. It works with the full card only:
the compact layout is a single row, and combining the two asserts.
`FlowChatView` docks its composer at its natural height, so `expands` is
for a composer you place and size yourself.

## Send and stop

`isStreaming` drives the send button: `false` shows send, `true` swaps it
for stop and routes taps to `onStop`. Flip it from your generation state
and the composer stays honest about what a tap will do.

Name the two buttons with `sendTooltip` and `stopTooltip`. flow_ui ships
no copy, so the words are yours: they show on hover and are what a screen
reader announces, and both buttons read as buttons with their enabled
state.

## Typing

On hardware keyboards **Enter sends** and **Shift+Enter** inserts a
newline — `submitOnEnter: false` opts out; mobile soft keyboards keep
their newline key and send from the button. While an input method is
composing, Enter confirms the composition instead of sending, and while a
reply streams it does nothing rather than adding a newline. The field grows with the draft
to `maxLines` (6) and scrolls beyond it, and `clearOnSend` empties it
after a successful send. `onSend` receives the trimmed text, and fires
empty only when something is attached — a picture with no caption is
still a message. In the [compact](#compact) layout the field holds a
single line, and the pill opens into the full card the moment the draft
wraps.

## Attach

Three ways to open the platform's file dialog, and they differ only in
who owns the control. All three hand you decoded `FlowAttachment`s;
holding them is still yours — add them to state and pass them back
through `attachments`.

**From your own control** — the design's "+" menu — call
`showFlowAttachmentPicker`. It resolves empty when the dialog is
dismissed or everything was refused, and it never throws: a dialog that
cannot open comes back through `onRejected` under an empty name. Call it
synchronously from the tap — inside `onSelected`, not a post-frame
callback — because on the web the dialog needs the gesture's user
activation.

```dart title="The design's route: 'Add Files or Photos' in the + menu"
FlowComposer(
  onSend: send,
  attachments: pending,
  onRemoveAttachment: removePending,
  leadingActions: [
    FlowMenu(
      icon: Icons.add,
      entries: const [FlowMenuOption(id: 'files', label: 'Add Files or Photos')],
      onSelected: (id) {
        if (id == 'files') {
          showFlowAttachmentPicker(options: options, onRejected: reject)
              .then((picked) => setState(() => pending.addAll(picked)));
        }
      },
    ),
  ],
)
```

**From a built-in button** — `onAttachmentsPicked` renders an attach
button first in the action row and opens the same dialog from it:

```dart title="The package renders the button and opens the dialog"
FlowComposer(
  onSend: send,
  onAttachmentsPicked: (picked) => setState(() => pending.addAll(picked)),
  attachTooltip: 'Attach images',
  attachments: pending,
  onRemoveAttachment: removePending,
)
```

**Picking yourself** — `onAttach` renders that same button as a plain
intent, for a photo sheet, a camera, a menu of sources, anything the
package has no business knowing about; whatever you gather goes back
through `attachments` the same way.

```dart title="Or own the picking"
FlowComposer(
  onSend: send,
  onAttach: () async {
    final file = await openGallerySheet();   // your picker
    if (file != null) setState(() => pending.add(toAttachment(file)));
  },
  attachTooltip: 'Attach files',
  attachments: pending,
  onRemoveAttachment: removePending,
)
```

Passing neither of the last two leaves the button off. Passing both
asserts — one button cannot have two owners.

### Turning it off

Presence of a callback says the feature is *wired*; `attachmentsEnabled`
says it is *available*. Set it false — for a plan tier, a model that
takes no images, a thread that has closed — and every way in stops at
once without unwiring anything: no attach button, no paste, no keyboard
media, and a drop on the card is swallowed — the card stays a drop zone
as far as the browser is concerned, showing the refusing cursor, so a
file dropped on it never navigates the tab away. `FlowChatView` has the same flag for the
surface's drop, so a host flips both together. Attachments already
pending stay in the strip and can still be removed, and a `+` menu entry
that calls `showFlowAttachmentPicker` is yours to hide alongside it.

```dart title="Attachments wired, but off for this plan"
FlowComposer(
  attachmentsEnabled: plan.allowsUploads,
  onAttachmentsPicked: addAttachments,
  onAttachmentsPasted: addAttachments,
  ...
)
```

### What is accepted

`attachmentOptions` says what the dialog offers and how what comes back
is decoded. It defaults to images, with the full-screen preview capped at
2048px and no file-size limit — a cap is policy, and policy is yours.
Tiles need no cap of their own: each one decodes to its own size and the
display's pixel ratio.

```dart
const FlowAttachmentOptions(
  allowMultiple: true,
  maxFileSize: 10 * 1024 * 1024,
  accept: [FlowAttachmentTypeGroup.images],
)
```

A `FlowAttachmentTypeGroup` names its types four ways — `extensions`,
`mimeTypes`, `uniformTypeIdentifiers`, `webWildCards` — because the
platforms disagree and each one throws when the family it reads is empty:
Apple platforms want the type identifiers, Windows wants the extensions,
Linux and Android take either extensions or MIME types, the web wants the
wildcards. Fill in all four for a group that works everywhere, the way
`FlowAttachmentTypeGroup.images` does.

Anything the options refuse is reported to `onAttachmentRejected` with
the file's name and a `FlowAttachmentRejection` — `tooLarge`,
`unsupportedType`, `unreadable`. flow_ui ships no copy for these, so
saying so is yours, and so is the wording.

### Saying so

Whatever the refusal — or any other error the composer should own up to
— `errorMessage` raises the design's banner: a tab in the error wash
tucked behind the card, with a warning glyph and your line beside it,
wrapping from the glyph when long. The words are yours, and so is
clearing it: it stays until the cross at its trailing edge fires
`onErrorDismiss` and you set it back to null — or until you clear it on
your own terms, in which case leave `onErrorDismiss` null and no cross
is drawn.

```dart title="The error banner"
FlowComposer(
  onSend: send,
  onAttachmentRejected: (name, reason) => setState(() {
    rejection = '$name is not an image';
  }),
  errorMessage: rejection,
  onErrorDismiss: () => setState(() => rejection = null),
  errorDismissTooltip: 'Dismiss',
)
```

`errorIcon` swaps the drawn diamond for an icon of your own set;
`FlowComposerStyle.errorBackgroundColor` and `errorForegroundColor`
recolour the tab.

<Aside type="caution" title="macOS needs an entitlement">
The built-in picker uses `file_selector`, which on macOS needs
`com.apple.security.files.user-selected.read-only` in **both**
`macos/Runner/DebugProfile.entitlements` and
`macos/Runner/Release.entitlements`. Without it the dialog opens onto
nothing and no error is raised. Release is the one people forget.
</Aside>

On iOS and Android the dialog is the system **file** browser, not the
photo gallery — that is the cost of staying on flutter.dev packages, and
exactly the case `onAttach` exists for.

### Drop on the card

`onAttachmentsDropped` makes the card itself a drop target. While a file
is over it the card lights up — the hairline goes solid and the fill
takes a 6% wash, both in `FlowComposerStyle.dropHighlightColor`
(`primary` by default) — and letting go hands back the same decoded
`FlowAttachment`s the picker does, held to the same `attachmentOptions`.

```dart title="Drop scoped to the composer"
FlowComposer(
  onSend: send,
  onAttachmentsPicked: (picked) => setState(() => pending.addAll(picked)),
  onAttachmentsDropped: (dropped) => setState(() => pending.addAll(dropped)),
  attachments: pending,
  onRemoveAttachment: removePending,
)
```

There are **two places to wire a drop**, and they differ only in scope:

| | Catches a drop | Treatment |
|---|---|---|
| `FlowComposer.onAttachmentsDropped` | over the composer card | the card lights up |
| [`FlowChatView.onAttachmentsDropped`](/components/chat-view/#drag-and-drop) | anywhere on the surface | full-bleed tint and invitation card |

The surface is the forgiving target and what most chat apps want; the
card is for an app where the rest of the page has its own meaning for a
dropped file. Wiring **both** is fine — the innermost target under the
pointer wins, so the card takes drops aimed at it and the surface takes
the rest.

<Aside type="caution" title="Drop is web-only">
The Flutter SDK implements OS file drop on the **web** and nowhere else.
Off the web `onAttachmentsDropped` compiles and never fires, and a debug
build prints a line saying so once — which is why the attach button, not
the drop, is what carries this feature on every platform.
</Aside>

### Paste

`onAttachmentsPasted` takes an image pasted into the field — Ctrl+V or
Cmd+V — and hands it back decoded, held to the same `attachmentOptions`
as everything else.

```dart title="Three ways in, one handler"
FlowComposer(
  onSend: send,
  onAttachmentsPicked: addAttachments,
  onAttachmentsDropped: addAttachments,
  onAttachmentsPasted: addAttachments,
  attachments: pending,
  onRemoveAttachment: removePending,
)
```

It fires only while the field has focus, so a paste meant for another
input on the page is left alone, and only when the clipboard actually
holds a file — pasting text stays the field's own business, untouched.
When the clipboard holds **both**, which is what copying a file in Finder
or Explorer produces, the file is attached and the filename that would
otherwise have landed in the draft is suppressed.

<Aside type="caution" title="Paste is web-only too">
Flutter's `Clipboard` reads plain text and nothing else, on every
platform — `ClipboardData` has a single `text` variant, and its own
documentation says images may come "in the future". There is nothing for
the package to read off the web, so `onAttachmentsPasted` never fires
there and a debug build says so once.

Android is the near miss: media pasted through the **keyboard** rather
than the system clipboard arrives on `onContentInserted` below, so wiring
both covers more ground than either alone.
</Aside>

### Keyboard media

On Android, `onContentInserted` receives media the software keyboard
inserts — a GIF picked inside Gboard — as mime type plus bytes, the one
media path the Flutter SDK covers without a plugin. It is IME insertion
only, not clipboard paste; the bytes arrive with no file behind them, so
build the `FlowAttachment` yourself and add it to `attachments`.

## Attachments

The composer renders a pending-attachments row above the field — pass
`attachments:` and `onRemoveAttachment:`. The [Attachments](/components/attachments/)
page shows the full flow, from the "+" menu to the sent message.

## Restyling

`FlowComposerStyle` overrides the composer's colors without touching the
global tokens: `backgroundColor` (the raised card's fill), `outlineColor`
(flattens the gradient hairline to one solid color in every state),
`sendBackgroundColor` and `sendForegroundColor` (the send/stop disc),
`textStyle` (merged over the field's default), `hintColor`,
`attachIconColor` (the attach button's resting glyph),
`dropHighlightColor` (what the card lights up in under a drag), and
`errorBackgroundColor` / `errorForegroundColor` (the error banner's wash
and ink). Install
one on `FlowTheme.composerStyle` for every composer; the widget's `style`
wins field by field.

## Key API

- `onSend` — required; receives the trimmed draft, never empty text.
- `isStreaming` + `onStop` — the send/stop swap; `sendTooltip` and
  `stopTooltip` name the two buttons.
- `enabled`, `submitOnEnter`, `clearOnSend`, `maxLines` — field behavior;
  all default on, with a 6-line growth cap.
- `layout` — `FlowComposerLayout.expanded`, the card and the default, or
  `FlowComposerLayout.compact`, the single-row pill that opens into the
  card as the draft grows and folds back once it is empty.
- `expands` — fills the height the parent gives the card, the field taking
  the space above the action row and scrolling inside it; needs a bounded
  height and the full card.
- `leadingActions` / `trailingActions` — widget slots on the input rail;
  any widget works, the flow_ui menus just fit the design out of the box.
- `attachments`, `onRemoveAttachment`, `removeAttachmentTooltip` — the
  pending row. `onAttachmentTap` *replaces* the built-in full-screen
  preview; `previewCloseTooltip` labels the preview's close button.
- `showFlowAttachmentPicker` — opens the platform's dialog from your own
  control and hands back decoded attachments; never throws.
  `onAttachmentsPicked` — renders a built-in attach button that opens the
  same dialog. `onAttach` — renders that button and leaves the picking to
  you; the two are mutually exclusive, and neither one leaves the button
  off.
  `attachmentOptions` + `onAttachmentRejected` — what is accepted, and
  what wasn't. `attachTooltip` names the button.
  `onAttachmentsDropped` — drag-and-drop scoped to the card (web only);
  `FlowChatView` has the whole-surface version.
  `onAttachmentsPasted` — images pasted into the field (web only).
  `attachmentsEnabled` — turns every way in off at once, handlers left
  wired.
  `errorMessage` + `errorIcon` — the error banner above the card;
  `onErrorDismiss` + `errorDismissTooltip` draw its cross.
  `onContentInserted` — Android keyboard media insertion.
- `controller`, `focusNode`, `placeholder` — the field itself; pass your
  own `TextEditingController` to prefill or clear the draft.
  `placeholder` defaults to 'How can I help you today?' — the one string
  the package ships — so localized hosts pass their own copy, and an
  explicit null renders no hint.
- `padding`, `borderRadius` — the card's metric overrides (the design's
  24px corner by default).
